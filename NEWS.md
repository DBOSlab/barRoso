## News

# barRoso (development version)

### New functions

- `barroso_dwc()`: formats a dataset into Darwin Core column names and values. It recognizes the three layouts of speciesLink downloads (e.g. `collector`, `recordedby`, or proper Darwin Core names), and records from REFLORA, JABOT and GBIF. For speciesLink, it also builds `dateIdentified` from the year, month and day of identification, adds the author to `scientificName`, splits epithets like `"cf. platychila"` or `"stygium var. annulatum"`, and takes a missing `recordNumber` from `fieldNumber`. `barroso_cat()` now runs it on each source.
- `std_identifiedBy()`: standardizes determiner names in `identifiedBy` with the same rules of `std_recordedBy()`, keeping all determiners in the same cell (e.g. `"D. Cardoso & L. P. Queiroz"`) and removing herbarium acronyms and determination dates. It is now part of `barroso_std()` (new argument `colname_identifiedBy`).
- `std_coordinates()`: fixes transposed coordinates and flags empty, out of range, country-inconsistent and low precision coordinates, as well as coordinates at capitals, centroids, institutions and zero coordinates, following the workflow of the bdc package. Flags use the bdc columns (e.g. `.coordinates_empty`), plus a readable `coordinateIssues` column.

- `barroso_fix_names()`: fixes misspelled names and synonyms of herbarium records into accepted names, using the World Checklist of Vascular Plants (through expowo) as a global backbone and, by default, the Flora e Funga do Brasil (through floraR, `flora_brasil = TRUE`), which is preferred for the names it has. Each unique binomial is searched once, misspelled names and genera are matched to the closest name (`max_distance`), and the columns `taxonOriginal`, `taxonStatus` and `taxonSource` are added. With `vascular_only = TRUE`, records of families not in WCVP (e.g. mosses, fungi and algae) are removed. It is meant to be run after `barroso_cat()` and before `barroso_std()`. On the 4,037 REFLORA and JABOT records of *Ormosia*, 10 misspelled names, 10 synonyms and 1 misspelled synonym are updated in 14 seconds.

- `dms_to_decimal()`: converts coordinates in degrees, minutes and seconds (e.g. `"22º22´55´´S"`, `"53°45'W"`, `"43º40'49''O"`) into decimal degrees, recognizing the many symbols and hemisphere letters (also in Portuguese) found in herbarium data. Invalid values (e.g. 61 seconds) become `NA`, with a warning.

- `std_dates()`: standardizes the day, month and year of collection into whole numbers, e.g. `"06"`, `"6.0"` and `"Jun"`, `"junho"` or `"VI"` into `6`, and zeros like `"00"` into `NA`. Days and months in the wrong columns are swapped when the month is greater than 12 (e.g. month `23` and day `7`, or REFLORA dates like `"4/16/2003"`), missing values are taken from `eventDate`, and impossible dates become `NA` (e.g. month `18`, or day `31` in April). The new column `dateIssues` tells what was changed. It is now part of `barroso_std()`, before duplicates are found by their dates, so `"5"` and `"05"` no longer separate duplicates.

- `barroso_flag_specialists()`: flags the records identified by specialists, given in a data frame with their taxa of expertise (family, genus or species), in the new columns `specialistIdentification`, `specialistName` and `specialistEvidence`: identified by the specialist, collected by them without determiner (also as additional collector), or a duplicate with the same name of a specimen identified by them. With `filter = TRUE`, only these records are kept, e.g. for distribution maps or niche models. It also runs in `barroso_std()` with the new argument `flag_specialists = TRUE`, before duplicates are removed, so a record kept from a block is flagged when a removed duplicate was identified by a specialist. On 10,294 *Ormosia* records, 1,072 were identified by two specialists of the genus.

- `barroso_std()`: new argument `database`, a database already standardized by `barroso_std()` and corrected by hand (a data frame or an .xlsx, .csv or .rds file), to which new downloads are added without standardizing it again. New records already in the database are removed by their identifiers (`gbifID` or `occurrenceID` of the same `datasource`) before being standardized, and then by their catalog numbers, also those of duplicates removed before (`duplicateCatalogNumbers`), or by all their fields when without catalog number. New duplicates of the specimens of the database are added to their duplicate columns (and removed with `rm_duplicates = TRUE`), keeping the names of the database. The new records are marked with `newRecord`. Updating an *Ormosia* database of 6,755 records with the same sources downloaded again, plus 300 GBIF records left out before, added just 41 new records, all of them from those 300, in a few seconds.

### Improvements

- `barroso_std()`: new argument `match_collectors` (default `TRUE`), a deep search across the whole data, before finding duplicates, for the same collector written in other ways, e.g. `"L. Queiroz"` and `"L. P. Queiroz"`, or `"A. D. Salinas"`, `"A. Delgado Salinas"` and `"A. Delgado-Salinas"`, which are compared by their initials and last surname, the first surnames of compound surnames as initials. Records with the same first initial, surname and collector number get the most complete name only when they have the same date or municipality, so `"L. Queiroz 12456"` and `"L. P. Queiroz 12456"` of different dates and places are kept as two people. When several complete names fit, the one that agrees the most with the record (same full date, year and month, year, municipality) is chosen, e.g. `"E. H. A. Rodrigues"` rather than `"E. M. A. Rodrigues"` for `"E. A. Rodrigues"` of the same date; names that fit two different complete names equally (`"L. Queiroz"` with `"L. P. Queiroz"` and `"L. M. Queiroz"`) are kept, and surnames are never turned into initials (`"D. Cardoso"` is not `"D. Queiroz Cardoso"`). For the same initials written in other ways, the most frequent name is kept, with accents. On 10,294 *Ormosia* records, 205 records of 82 names got a more complete name, e.g. `"R. Fróes"` into `"R. L. Fróes"`, and 183 more records were found among duplicates. It is also used to find the duplicates of new records in a database.

- `barroso_cat()`: the identifiers of the records in their sources (`occurrenceID`, and `gbifID` in GBIF) are kept. Records without a unique identifier, from any source (e.g. all speciesLink downloads made with plantR, or a few GBIF records without `gbifID`), are compared with a database by their catalog numbers instead, and a message tells how many there are.

- `barroso_cat()`: `keep_source` is now required, with the names of all sources in order of priority, e.g. `c("JABOT", "speciesLink", "REFLORA", "GBIF")`. The herbaria of each source are removed from the sources after it, so each herbarium is kept from a single source, the first that has it. Before, only the herbaria of one preferred source were removed from the others, so herbaria shared by the other sources (e.g. speciesLink and JABOT) were kept twice. On the *Ormosia* records of the four sources, with GBIF first, 173 records of 23 herbaria shared by JABOT and speciesLink are no longer repeated.
- `std_recordedBy()`: all additional collectors are kept in `addCollector`, each one standardized with the rules of the main collector and joined as `"A. Alves, K. Paixão & E. Consuelo"`, instead of just `"et al."`, which is now written only when it is in the original names, e.g. `"J.A. Lombardi, H. Lorenzi, R. Tsuji et al."` into `"H. Lorenzi & R. Tsuji et al."`. Names separated by semicolons, commas, `|` or nothing (`"V. Ariati D.A. Roher J.E. Bianchin"`), with the surname first (`"Torezan, JMD"`) or last, in lowercase (`"Douglas daly"`) or followed by particles (`"Lima, H.C. de"`) are recognized. On the 7,141 collector names of *Ormosia*, 1,739 records now have all their additional collectors. Names with the surname first whose initials are glued to the next name are split, e.g. `"Cardoso, D.Queiroz, L.P.; Jardim, J.G."` into `"D. Cardoso"` and `"L. P. Queiroz & J. G. Jardim"` (before `"D. Queiroz Cardoso"`), fixing 9 collector strings of *Ormosia*. Other forms of the same name between parentheses, e.g. `"Berg, C. C. (Berg, Cornelis Christiaan)"`, are removed, and the misspelled particle `"dso"` is read as `"dos"`.
- `barroso_flag_duplicates()` and `barroso_std()`: `specialists` is now a data frame with the columns `specialist` and `taxon`, the family, genus or species of expertise of each specialist (one row for each taxon), e.g. `data.frame(specialist = c("D. B. O. S. Cardoso", "H. C. Lima"), taxon = c("Ormosia", "Leguminosae"))`. A specialist counts only in blocks with a name of their taxa, and families are compared with `family`, also by their alternative names (e.g. Leguminosae and Fabaceae). Records without determiner collected by a specialist count also when the specialist is an additional collector (`addCollector`, new argument `colname_addCollector`).
- `barroso_write_specimens()`: `catalog_numbers = TRUE` is now the default, and all catalog numbers of each herbarium are written, with the herbaria separated by semicolons, e.g. `"(NY barcode 5464648; MO barcodes 45556667, 000894546)"` in Taxon or `"(MO 45556667, 000894546; NY 5464648)"` in Systematic Botany. Several additional collectors are written as `et al.`.

- `barroso_flag_duplicates()`: new column `identificationUnresolved`, `TRUE` for the records of blocks with conflicting identifications whose name cannot be chosen, as their names are equally frequent (e.g. two duplicates as *Ormosia arborea* and two as *Ormosia fastigiata*), even after `specialists`. It marks the conflicts that need checking, to be found with a single filter, e.g. `subset(df, identificationUnresolved %in% TRUE)`, whether or not duplicates are removed.
- `barroso_flag_duplicates()` and `barroso_std()`: with `rm_duplicates = TRUE`, conflicting identifications are no longer resolved at random. With the new argument `specialists` (e.g. `c("D. B. O. S. Cardoso", "H. C. Lima")`), the duplicates identified by them, or collected by them when without determiner, are kept, whatever the number of duplicates with other names. When names are still equally frequent (e.g. two duplicates as *Ormosia arborea* and two as *Ormosia fastigiata*), all records of the block are kept, to be checked, as needed in taxonomic revisions and checklists; with the new argument `rm_tied_conflicts = TRUE`, as may suit macroecological analyses, the whole block is removed. A message tells how many records and blocks were kept or removed, and why.
- `std_collection()`: catalog numbers in `catalogNumber` are written without spaces and punctuation, with the standardized acronym of the herbarium when they are just numbers, e.g. `"181351"` into `"HUA181351"`, `"HATM 003653"` into `"HATM003653"`, `"ANDES-H:6594"` into `"ANDESH6594"`, `"L.2049875"` into `"L2049875"`, `"CANB 490096.1"` into `"CANB4900961"` and `"barcode-00064442"` into `"A00064442"`. Collector names with numbers written as catalog numbers, e.g. `"M. A. Chocce 536"`, become `NA`, and identifiers like `"ec29d5d8-b6bf-11e1-8c2f-acd112e444a7"` are kept. The original catalog numbers are kept in `catalogNumberOriginal`. In `barroso_write_specimens()`, catalog numbers that already start with the acronym are written without it in styles like `"HUEFS no. 000123"`.
- `std_recordedBy()`: collector numbers followed by a single letter, also Greek or `ß`, keep the letter, e.g. `"Koorders, SH 5661 B"`, `"Koorders, SH 9588 ß"` and `"123 α"` into `"5661B"`, `"9588ß"` and `"123α"`. On the Ormosia dataset, 6 numbers like `"Kerr, AFG 19008 A"`, which became `"Kerr"`, are now right (`"19008A"`). Commas and dots between digits are removed, e.g. `"12,979b"` and `"1.350"` into `"12979b"` and `"1350"`, after removing the decimals of numbers saved by spreadsheets, e.g. `"159.0"` into `"159"`.
- `barroso_chars_template()`: new profile `plant_group = "Myristicaceae"`, with one sentence for each organ, as in Myristicaceae descriptions: Petiole, Leaf blade, Staminate and Pistillate inflorescences, Flower, Androecium, Ovary, Infructescence, Fruit and Seed, including the exudate, the leaf trichomes, the number of anthers, the pericarp thickness and the aril. A full description written from simulated specimens is in `data-raw/myristicaceae_example/`.
- `barroso_write_descriptions()`: organs of two words are joined by a hyphen, e.g. `LEAF-BLADE` and `STAMINATE-INFLORESCENCE` for *Leaf blade* and *Staminate inflorescence*, and terms like `SECONDARY-VEINS` are written without it (*secondary veins*). Maximum values written as `"up to 5"` become *up to 5 cm long*, thickness and diameter columns become *1–2 mm thick* and *3–4 mm in diam.*, and counts are followed by all the lowercase words at the end of the column name, e.g. *fascicles 5–15 per axis*.
- `barroso_write_descriptions()`: the traits of each part are written in the order of the columns, with the measurements where their first column is and sub-parts named once, e.g. *stamen 10, sessile, anthers brownish, wrinkled, glabrous, 5–6 × 1 mm* instead of *stamen 10, sessile, anthers brownish, anthers wrinkled, anthers glabrous, anthers 5–6 × 1 mm*.
- `barroso_write_descriptions()`: within each organ, single traits are now separated by commas, and parts with their own traits in lowercase start after a semicolon, e.g. `"Shrub or tree ca. 1.8 m tall, erect, branched; stem striated, scaly, gray, glabrous. Stipule 1 pair, lateral to petiole, 11–16 × 2–3 mm, lanceolate, base truncate, margin entire, apex acute or aristate, glabrous."`, instead of semicolons between all traits.
- `barroso_write_descriptions()`: columns of counts, with just numbers (e.g. `LEAF SECONDARY-VENATION pairs` with `"6 | 8 | 12"`), are pooled into ranges with rare values between parentheses, as the measurements, followed by the last word of the column name (e.g. `"6–12 pairs"`, but `"stamen 8–10"` for `FLOWER STAMEN number`). In `chars_dataset`, the pairs of secondary veins are now in their own column, and `INFLORESCENCE flower number` (`"with around 70 flowers"`) is now `INFLORESCENCE flowers` (`"70"`).
- `chars_dataset` (formerly `morphological_dataset`) now has the measurements of the latest *Ouratea* spreadsheet, with several measurements of the same specimen separated by `|`, so *Ouratea hostilis* shows rare leaf blade lengths between parentheses: `(4.3–)4.7–13(–14.5) × 1–4.1 cm`.
- `barroso_write_descriptions()`: rare small and large measurements are written between parentheses, as usual in taxonomic descriptions, e.g. `"(2–)6–40(–55) m"`. Values marked in the spreadsheet, e.g. `"(5–)7–38(–45)"`, are always kept, and with the new arguments `extremes` (default `c(0.1, 0.9)`) and `extremes_min_n` (default `10`), the values below the 10th and above the 90th percentile of a species with at least 10 values are written between parentheses, while the range limits are always measured values. Several measurements of the same specimen are written in the same cell, separated by `|`, e.g. `"4.5 | 7 | 11 | 14"`.
- All functions now check their arguments in the same way, with clear errors that name the argument to fix, e.g. "`rm_duplicates` must be TRUE or FALSE.", "`df` must be a data frame, not NULL.", or "Column(s) not found in `df`: 'recordedBy'. If they have other names, set `colname_recordedBy`.". `barroso_write_descriptions()` now checks the file before creating the output folder.
- `remove_authorship()` is now `rm_authors()`, `barroso_add_char_template()` is now `barroso_chars_template()`, `barroso_write_taxon_descr()` is now `barroso_write_descriptions()`, and the example dataset `morphological_dataset` is now `chars_dataset`.
- `barroso_flag_duplicates()` used on its own now treats empty collector numbers (`""` or `"NA"`) as missing, so they are no longer taken as duplicates.

- `std_types()`: the same type designation in English, Latin or Portuguese and in any case becomes one lowercase Darwin Core term, e.g. `"ISOTYPE"`, `"Isotypus"` and `"isótipo"` into `"isotype"`, or `"Parátipo"` into `"paratype"`, keeping a taxon name after the term. On the Ormosia and Dalea datasets, 58 spellings become 25.
- `barroso_std()`: cells with the text `"NA"`, as in spreadsheets exported from R, are now missing values, except in code columns like `countryCode`, where `"NA"` is a real code (Namibia).
- `barroso_cat()`: standardizes records of REFLORA and JABOT (as returned by refloraR and jabotR): the determiner `"Administrador"` of REFLORA becomes `NA`, dates become ISO 8601 (e.g. `"2/12/1940"` into `"1940-12-02"`), REFLORA type status in Latin becomes Darwin Core terms (`"ISOTYPUS"` into `"isotype"`), and the institution of RB becomes `"JBRJ"`.

- `barroso_write_specimens()`: rewritten to write the lists of examined specimens of one, several or all species (`species`) of a database in Darwin Core format, given as a data frame (`df`) or a file (`path`, .xlsx or .csv), in the format of 24 journals (`journal`): Systematic Botany, Acta Amazonica, Acta Botanica Brasilica, Acta Botanica Mexicana, Annals of the Missouri Botanical Garden, Botanical Journal of the Linnean Society, Botany Letters, Brazilian Journal of Botany, Brittonia, Candollea, Darwiniana, Edinburgh Journal of Botany, Feddes Repertorium, Hoehnea, Kew Bulletin, Nordic Journal of Botany, Phytotaxa, PhytoKeys, Plant Ecology and Evolution, Plant Systematics and Evolution, Rodriguésia, Systematics and Biodiversity, Taxon and Willdenowia. Each format was checked against recent papers of the journal: the header, case and format (bold, italics, small capitals) of countries, states and collectors, dates (e.g. `"8 May 1994"`, `"8.V.1994"`, `"8 v 1994"`), coordinates, phenology, collector names (`"D.B.O.S. Cardoso"`, `"D. B. O. S. Cardoso"` or `"Cardoso"`), `"et al."` or `"& al."`, and herbaria (with catalog numbers with `catalog_numbers = TRUE`), without type status. The phenology (column `phenology`, as in records from REFLORA and JABOT, or `reproductiveCondition`, e.g. `"fl"`, `"flower, fruit"` or `"st"`) is written in the format of each journal, e.g. `"(fl, fr)"`, `"(fl., fr.)"`, `"(fl./fr.)"`, `"[fl]"` or `"fl. and fr."`. With `language`, the list is written in English (`"en"`), Portuguese (`"pt"`) or Spanish (`"es"`), e.g. for Hoehnea, Rodriguésia or Darwiniana: header (`"Material examinado"`), months, countries (`"BRASIL"`), phenology (`"fl. e fr."`) and west hemisphere (`"O"` in Spanish). With `one_per_municipality = TRUE`, just the most complete specimen of each municipality is written for each species (with state, locality, coordinates, elevation, date, phenology, collector and number, then the most herbaria), and specimens without municipality are left out; on the Ormosia dataset, 7,621 specimens of 122 species become 2,055 specimens of 105 species. Repeated places are written as in each journal, e.g. `"l.c."` in Hoehnea or `"loc. cit."` in Acta Botanica Mexicana. The header follows the journal (e.g. `"Specimens examined"` in Kew Bulletin), or any text given in `header` (e.g. `"Paratypes"`). Duplicates are written once with all their herbaria, and collections without number are cited with the catalog number of the first herbarium. The arguments `xlsx_path`, `species_cols`, `species_filter` and the `colname_*` and formatting arguments were removed.
- `std_collection()`: rewritten with regular expressions instead of a list of exact codes. Aggregated collections (`"Tropicos"`, `"MO-Duplicates"`) take the herbarium of the specimen from `institutionCode`; generic words (`"Botany"`, `"Herbarium"`, `"Vascular Plants"`) take the acronym from `institutionCode`, `catalogNumber` (e.g. `"U.1317533"`, `"D. Cardoso 2118 (HUEFS)"`) or `occurrenceID`; names of collections become the acronym (`"F-Seedplants"`, `"BHCB-Fanerogamas"`, `"R-Tipos"`, `"BSMvplantscoll"`); names of herbaria and institutions become their acronym (`"Herbario INPA"`, `"Herbario Nacional de Costa Rica (CR)"`, `"Instituto Florestal"` into `"SPSF"`); and codes of observation projects, companies or numbers become `NA`. On the Ormosia dataset, 367 codes become 298 acronyms.
- `std_place()`: words for municipality in `county` and `municipality` are removed, leaving just the name, e.g. `"Manaus Mun."`, `"Mun. de Castelo"`, `"Município de Mucugê"` or `"Arriaga Municipio"` into `"Manaus"`, `"Castelo"`, `"Mucugê"` and `"Arriaga"`. Particles are written in lowercase, e.g. `"Rio Grande Do Norte"` into `"Rio Grande do Norte"`. Country codes become names (e.g. `"BR"`, `"GY"` or `"BRA"` into `"Brazil"` and `"Guyana"`). Missing countries are taken from the Brazilian state (e.g. `"Bahia"`), from a country name at the start or end of the locality (e.g. `"Rio Negro, Brasil"`), or from other records with the same state and county (e.g. `"Amazonas"`, `"Manaus"`), and missing municipalities from the county. Unknown counties like `"inc.sed.cty."` become `NA`. On the Ormosia dataset, 53 country codes are converted, 35 missing countries are found, and missing municipalities go from 6,848 to 3,884. Localities all in upper case get just the first letter of each word in upper case, e.g. `"ESTR. UBAITUBA / MARÁU AO KM 54 ."` into `"Estr. Ubaituba/Maráu ao Km 54."`, keeping prepositions in lowercase and road codes (`"BR-316"`), acronyms (`"ESALQ"`) and compass points (`"10 Km NE de"`) in upper case.
- `barroso_std()`: the final data frame is now sorted by species, `recordedBy` and `recordNumber`, with numbers in numerical order (`"20"` before `"100"`). All records of a block of duplicates stay together under the most frequent name of the block, e.g. a duplicate identified as `"Ormosia ruddiana"` goes with its three duplicates identified as `"Ormosia amazonica"`, in the place of the latter species.
- `barroso_cat()`: the herbarium acronyms (`collectionCode`) of each source are standardized with `std_collection()` before comparing the sources, by default checking them against Index Herbariorum (new argument `index_herbariorum`), so the same herbarium is recognized in all sources (e.g. `"NL-Botany"`, `"MO-Duplicates"` and `"F-Seedplants"` in speciesLink as `"L"`, `"MO"` and `"F"` in GBIF). The original codes are kept in `collectionCodeOriginal`, and `barroso_std()` still removes unvouchered collections like `"INPA-Carpoteca"` by their original codes. On the Ormosia dataset of GBIF, speciesLink, REFLORA and JABOT, with `keep_source = "GBIF"`, 2,427 more records of herbaria already in GBIF are removed (16,415 records instead of 18,842). GBIF records citing the REFLORA Virtual Herbarium are no longer taken as REFLORA records by `barroso_dwc()`.
- `std_collection()` and `barroso_std()`: new argument `index_herbariorum`, `TRUE` by default (use `FALSE` to work offline). All herbaria of the [Index Herbariorum API](https://github.com/nybgvh/IH-API/wiki) are downloaded at once (kept during the R session) and the standardized `collectionCode` is checked against them. Codes found get the spelling of Index Herbariorum (e.g. `"UnB"` into `"UNB"`), and codes not found are looked for by the full name of the institution, when it has a single herbarium. `institutionCode` is not changed. New columns have the name of the institution (`institutionName`) and whether the code was found (`indexHerbariorum`), and the original code is always kept in `collectionCodeOriginal`. On the Ormosia dataset, 6,645 of 6,855 records are found. The regular expressions remain the first step, as dirty codes like `"F-Seedplants"` are not in Index Herbariorum.
- `barroso_std()`: with `unvouchered = TRUE`, observations without a specimen (`basisOfRecord` like `"HUMAN_OBSERVATION"`) are also removed, as well as wood, seed, fruit and pollen collections written in any case (e.g. `"BCTw"`, `"BHZBcarpo"`), now before the codes are standardized.
- `std_recordedBy()` and `std_identifiedBy()`: Chinese names written in Chinese characters (e.g. `"丁广奇、余莲"`), and not only as unicode escapes, are converted into Latin names (`"G. Q. Ding"` and `"L. Yu"`); when also written in Latin (e.g. `"K.C.Yang 楊國禎"`), the Latin name is kept.
- `std_recordedBy()`: removes Wikidata identifiers and codes like `"[DR-02688]"`, capitalizes given names in lowercase (`"katherine Bravo"`), abbreviates names like `"Edm.Pereira"`, fixes `"C. -I Peng"` into `"C.-I. Peng"`, and keeps names of teams and herbaria (e.g. `"Equipe do Jardim Botânico de Brasília"`, `"Belem Herbarium"`).
- `std_recordedBy()`: placeholders like `"-"`, `"?"`, `"[data not captured]"`, `"[missing; ex Herb. Acad. Petrop.]"`, `"[see remarks]"` or `"BLOQUEADO"` become `NA`, as well as empty cells, which were `"Unknown"`; titles and positions are removed (`"Col. Richard Henry Beddome"` into `"R. H. Beddome"`, `"Hno. Daniel"` into `"Daniel"`, `"Warren Douglas Stevens, B. A. Krukoff Curator of Central American Botany"` into `"W. D. Stevens"`); several collectors with the given names first are split (`"H.C. de Lima, C.A. Cid Ferreira, J. Guedes;F. Neves"` into `"H. C. Lima"` and `"et al."`, `"M. J. E. Coode D. W. Kirkup"` into `"M. J. E. Coode"` and `"D. W. Kirkup"`); and numbers of Chinese teams go to an empty `recordNumber` (`"236-6队"` into `"Dui"` and `"236-6"`), or are removed when `recordNumber` is filled. With `delunkcoll = TRUE`, `barroso_std()` also removes records with `NA` collector.
- `std_identifiedBy()`: determiners with just punctuation like `"-"` or `"?"`, and placeholders like `"[data not captured]"`, become `NA`.
- `std_recordedBy()` was refactored into small internal helpers (about half of the code), with the same results for all non-Chinese names of the Ormosia GBIF and speciesLink test datasets, and about twice as fast.
- `std_recordedBy()`: Chinese names in unicode escapes are now converted into abbreviated Latin names (e.g. `"G. Z. Li"`, as Chinese authors cite themselves), without the `tmcn` package, which is no longer a dependency.
- `std_recordedBy()`: fixed capitalization of accented names (e.g. `"DuséN"`), initials followed by particles (`"Martius, CFP von"`), three collectors like `"A. Gómez Pompa, A. J. Sharp & P. Hernández"`, remains of `"et al."`, HTML entities, and abbreviations that depended on the other names in the data.
- `barroso_flag_duplicates()`: groups duplicates into blocks (`duplicateGroup`), flags blocks with conflicting identifications (`identificationConflict`, `duplicateIdentifications`), and stores all herbaria and catalog numbers of each block (`duplicateCollectionCodes`, e.g. `"MO | JBB"`; `duplicateCatalogNumbers`, e.g. `"MO 2839102 | JBB 13548"`). With `rm_duplicates = TRUE`, the record kept is identified to species, has the most frequent identification of the block, and then has coordinates and more filled columns. Records with unknown collector are no longer grouped as duplicates.
- `barroso_cat()`: renames raw speciesLink columns into Darwin Core terms, combines columns with different types, adds a `datasource` column, and with `keep_source` removes only the herbaria also present in the preferred source.

### Bug fixes

- `std_collection()`: with custom column names, the herbarium column was renamed wrongly, so no cleaning was done, and the `genus` column was renamed.
- `std_place()`: the original municipality and locality columns were saved as `countyOriginal.1` and `countyOriginal.2`; they are now `municipalityOriginal` and `localityOriginal`.
- `std_place()`: `"French Guiana"` became `"Guyana"` when standardized again, and `"Guiane française"` was not recognized.
- `std_place()`: Venezuela was renamed "Venezula", and continents were not added when the continent column was completely empty.
- `std_place()`: USA state acronyms were never expanded, and their table was misaligned (e.g. `AK` would become `Alabama`); Brazilian acronyms were applied to records of all countries (e.g. `AL` in the USA became `Alagoas`); US records got no continent; and with a custom country column, states and continents were not standardized.
- `barroso_write_descriptions()`: trait values `black`, and whole numbers from 8 to 16 stored as numbers (e.g. 10 stamens), were silently dropped from the descriptions. A `filename` with the `.docx` extension is now saved in the output folder.
- `barroso_write_specimens()`: failed with any dataset with coordinates; missing type status, month, number and municipality were printed as `NA`; coordinates could show `60″`; and duplicates with different type status now keep each one, e.g. `[holotype HUEFS, isotype RB]`.
- `barroso_labels()`: a specimen not found in LCVP (e.g. `Ormosia sp.`) got the name of the previous label; authorities of infraspecific names are shown correctly.
- `barroso_std()`: failed with more than 10,000 records when the last chunk had a single record.
- `std_recordedBy()`: numbers before the collector name (e.g. `8470 G.H. Turner`) are moved to `recordNumber`.
- `std_taxa()`: `sp.`, `cf.` and `aff.` at the beginning of the epithet, and genera in capital letters, are now standardized.
- `rm_authors()`: removes all authors, e.g. `Ormosia arborea (Vell.) Harms` and `Swartzia apetala Raddi var. glabra (Vogel) R.S.Cowan`, so `species_filter` now matches these names.

# barRoso 1.0.0

## Initial Release

The first official release of the `barroso` R package — a comprehensive toolkit for standardizing, harmonizing, and preparing plant specimen records for research and reconciliation.

### Highlights

- `barroso_std()`: Unified function to clean and standardize herbarium records across multiple fields (collector, geography, taxonomy, etc.).
- `barroso_flag_duplicates()`: Flag potential duplicate specimens across herbaria using metadata patterns.
- `barroso_cat()`: Combine and reconcile specimen records from multiple virtual herbaria (e.g., GBIF, SEINet, REFLORA, JABOT, speciesLink).
- `barroso_labels()`: Generate printable herbarium labels from cleaned fieldbook data, with embedded maps and taxonomic authority retrieval.
- Standardize collector names and collection numbers using regex-based parsing
- Harmonize taxonomic, geographic, and temporal fields
- Flag and remove potential duplicates across herbarium records
- Generate herbarium labels from fieldbook data
- Integrate with external taxonomic databases (e.g., LCVP, WFO)
- Prepare large-scale biodiversity datasets for publication and analysis
- Optimized for datasets from [REFLORA](https://floradobrasil.jbrj.gov.br/reflora/herbarioVirtual/), [speciesLink](https://specieslink.net), and [JABOT](https://jabot.jbrj.gov.br/v3/consulta.php).
- Supports integration with tidyverse workflows for downstream analyses.
- Test coverage >95%, continuous integration via GitHub Actions.

### Philosophy

Unlike other tools that **aggressively clean (and discard)** records, `barroso` focuses on **standardization** first — ensuring that all specimens, even misidentified or ambiguous ones, remain usable and discoverable. Standardization also enables better duplicate detection and data reconciliation **without losing valuable information**.
